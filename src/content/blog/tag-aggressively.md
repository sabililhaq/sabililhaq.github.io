I'm forgetful as hell. If I don't write things down properly, they're gone.

But even when I write things down, I still often forget what I need to look up in my "second brain."

What actually saved me: **tag aggressively**. And I mean *aggressively;* 6, 8, 10 tags if it feels right. There's almost no downside, seriously.

When I find something useful, I don't just save it, I tag it from multiple angles, at different levels of abstraction. That way, even a vague memory is enough to find it later.

For example:

I found [monosketch.io](https://monosketch.io/), a tool for making quick ASCII diagrams. I could've stopped at `ascii` and `drawing`. But I kept going: `diagramming`, `visualization`, `system design`, whatever popped into my head.

Months later, I wasn't thinking "ASCII tool". I was thinking "I need something for a system design doc." Searched `system design`, then found it instantly.

That's the point. More tags = more chances I actually find it again.

People might worry about messy notes or duplicates. Honestly? I'd rather do a quick cleanup once in a while than lose stuff forever. There's even AI now to help clean up messy notes. My forgetful ass would rather have chaotic info than nothing at all.

So how do I come up with tags? Two things:

**1. Understand it well enough to abstract it.** If I can't describe something in multiple ways, I probably don't understand it yet. Tagging forces that clarity.

**2. Assume future me is clueless.** I write as if a stranger with zero context will read this later. That pushes me to add more varied, useful tags.

I pair this with daily journaling, brain-dump everything first, then hit it hard with tags. Over time, my past self becomes way more reliable than my memory.

Journaling + aggressive tagging is a high-ROI habit for forgetful people like me, might work for you too.
